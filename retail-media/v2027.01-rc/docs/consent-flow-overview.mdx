## Overview

There are two ways to set up consent in the Criteo Retail Media API. The right choice depends on your role and how your demand accounts are managed. Picking the wrong flow requires redoing your integration, so use the table below to confirm your setup before you start.

## Which Flow Applies to You?

| | Standard Flow | Private Market Flow |
|---|---|---|
| **Who uses it** | Third-party advertisers, agencies, or tech partners accessing a Criteo account | Retailers managing their own brands and marketplace sellers programmatically |
| **How consent is granted** | Advertiser clicks a consent URL and approves access through the Criteo portal | Retailer's Admin app grants consent directly via API — no manual step for the brand or seller |
| **Apps required** | One app (Authorization Code or Client Credentials) | Two apps: an Admin app (Client Credentials) and a Business app (Authorization Code) |
| **Who controls access** | The advertiser or agency granting access | The retailer |
| **Typical use case** | A brand or agency wants to manage their own campaigns via API | A retailer onboards brands or sellers onto their private market and manages consent on their behalf |

## Which One Am I?

**Use the Standard Flow if:**
- You are a brand, agency, or tech partner accessing a Criteo advertiser account that belongs to someone else
- Consent is granted by the advertiser themselves, not by you

**Use the Private Market Flow if:**
- You are a retailer operating a private market
- You create and manage demand accounts (brands or sellers) on behalf of advertisers
- You want to grant consent programmatically without requiring advertisers to interact with the Criteo portal

<Warning>
  If you are a retailer with a private market setup, do not use the Standard Flow. It requires advertisers to manually approve access through the Criteo consent portal, which does not match the programmatic model used in private markets.
</Warning>
